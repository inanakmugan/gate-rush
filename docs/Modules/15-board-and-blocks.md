# Module 15 — Board and block visuals

**Assembly:** `GateRush.Runtime` and `GateRush.Editor` (tests in `GateRush.Tests`)
**Depends on:** Modules 11, 12, 13
**Phase:** 2.4a

---

## Responsibility

Replace the placeholder look of the board with the game's own art, in the
style of the reference game but drawn by us (D46). After this module the
board reads as a toy: a framed board on a dark background, glossy studded
blocks that read as one piece each, and gates that are coloured openings in
the frame.

In scope:

- An editor tool that generates the module's sprites as PNG files, with
  their import settings, from parameters in one asset.
- The background, the board frame, walls drawn as frame, and the empty
  floor.
- Blocks drawn as one rounded piece with thickness and studs; the
  axis-restriction arrow (M7) in place of the studs; layered blocks (M4)
  still showing the colour beneath.
- Gates as coloured, arrowed segments of the frame. Closed gates keep their
  2.1 look for now.
- The board filling the screen's width, with bands kept free above and below
  for the HUD.
- The block palette re-tuned to the new look.

Not in scope, deliberately: frozen, locked and key-carrying blocks,
shutters, generators, elevators and closed gates beyond keeping them
readable (2.4b); the HUD, the font, the restart button and every animation
(2.4c). Those keep their current placeholder drawing, placed correctly on
the new board.

Names are negotiable. That tile selection is plain, tested code, and that
the art is generated by a tool rather than drawn by hand, is not.

## Design decisions (owner)

### The art is generated, not drawn (D46)

- An editor menu item (for example *Gate Rush → Generate Art*) draws every
  sprite of this module into textures and writes them as PNGs under
  `Assets/Art/Generated/`. Its parameters — sizes, corner radius, stud size
  and spacing, lip height, highlight and shade strengths, arrow shape —
  live in one `ArtRecipe` asset, so the look is tuned there, not in code.
- The generator sets each texture's import settings through
  `TextureImporter` (sprite mode, pixels per unit, filter mode, compression,
  pivot, and any 9-slice border). Unity writes the `.meta` files; nothing
  writes one by hand.
- Generation is deterministic: the same recipe produces byte-identical PNGs,
  so a regenerated file only shows in version control when the recipe
  changed. The PNGs are committed; the build never runs the generator.
- Every sprite is drawn in greyscale and tinted at runtime, so one set of
  sprites serves every colour. What must not take the tint — the gloss on a
  stud, the white arrow on a gate — is its own sprite drawn on top.

### Blocks are one piece (quarter tiles)

- Each footprint cell is drawn as four quarter tiles. Each quarter looks at
  its two orthogonal neighbours and its diagonal neighbour on that corner,
  within the same footprint, and picks one of five pieces:
  - neither orthogonal neighbour in the block → **outer corner** (rounded);
  - only the one along x → **edge** running along x;
  - only the one along y → **edge** running along y;
  - both, and the diagonal too → **fill**;
  - both, but not the diagonal → **concave corner** (the inside of an L).
- The pieces are rotated or flipped to their quarter; the generator draws
  each piece once.
- The result reads as one rounded shape per block: straight blocks, squares
  and L shapes alike. The gap between two different blocks comes from the
  outer pieces, so `BlockCellRects` and its inset rectangles are retired.
- **Thickness.** Each block is drawn twice: a darker copy of the shape offset
  a little downward (the lip), then the face. The offset and darkening come
  from `RuntimeConfig`.
- **Studs.** Every cell carries a 2×2 grid of studs on its face, tinted with
  the block's colour, with an untinted gloss on top.
- **Axis restriction (M7).** A block restricted to one axis shows a large
  double-headed arrow along its axis, across the whole block, instead of
  studs.
- **Layered blocks (M4)** keep showing the colour beneath and the count, as
  in 2.1, drawn on top of the new face. Their final look is 2.4b's.

### The board

- **Background:** a vertical gradient in two configured colours, with a soft
  vignette, filling the camera.
- **Floor:** every playable cell is a dark floor tile with a faint grid line.
- **Frame:** a thick rounded border around the playable cells, drawn with the
  same five pieces as a block, from `FrameTiling`. Every **wall** cell is
  frame too, so a board with walls reads as a shaped board, not as grey
  squares. The frame has the same lip as a block.
- **Gates** replace the frame segment they sit on: a segment of the gate's
  width, in the gate's colour, with the block's face and lip, and a white
  arrow pointing out of the board at its middle. A closed gate (M2) keeps
  its current colourless bar and count, placed on the frame; 2.4b gives it
  ice.
- Generators and elevators keep their current drawing, placed on the new
  board.

### Layout

- The camera fits the board plus the frame to the screen's width, keeping
  `sideMarginCells` on each side, and keeps a top and a bottom band free
  for the HUD, each a fraction of the screen's height (see below). When the screen is too short for that,
  the height decides instead. Portrait is the target; landscape must still
  show the whole board.
- Re-fitted when the screen size changes, as today.

### Palette

- The eight `BlockColor` values keep their meaning; `RuntimeConfig`'s block
  palette is re-tuned toward saturated, glossy toy colours that stay
  distinct from each other and from the frame and background. The owner
  tunes the final values in the Inspector.

### Engine rules that carry over

- No magic numbers: every size, offset, colour and sorting order is in
  `RuntimeConfig` or `ArtRecipe`.
- Every sprite uses `Sprite-Unlit-Default`.
- The drag (Module 13), the settle and the clear effects (Module 12) keep
  working: they move a block's root, so the new pieces must all sit under
  it, and the exit effect's fade must reach every renderer of the block.
- WebGL is a target: generated textures use compression that works there,
  and the number of renderers per cell stays modest (quarters, lip, studs:
  about a dozen).

## Left to you

- How `ArtGenerator` draws: pixel loops, signed distance functions, or
  anything else, as long as it is deterministic and reads only `ArtRecipe`.
- Texture sizes and pixels per unit, within `ArtRecipe`.
- Whether the lip is a second set of quarter tiles or one offset copy of the
  face renderers.
- How the frame is laid out: for example as a ring of cells just outside the
  grid, tiled with `FrameTiling` together with the wall cells, and drawn at
  a configured thickness thinner than a cell. `FrameTiling`'s tests hold
  whichever way.
- How `BoardView` is restructured to draw the new pieces; keep the rebuild
  model of Module 11.
- Starting values for the palette and every new `RuntimeConfig` and
  `ArtRecipe` field. The owner tunes them.
- The asset steps the owner must do: list them precisely at the end,
  including running the generator and assigning the sprites.

## Tests

Edit Mode, against the plain classes. The look is checked by hand.

**`BlockTiling`**
- A 1×1 block is four outer corners.
- A horizontal 1×2 has outer corners at its ends and edges along x where
  the two cells meet; the vertical 1×2 mirrors it.
- A 2×2 has four outer corners and fills in the middle quarters.
- An L has exactly one concave corner, at the inside of the bend.
- Every cell gets exactly four quarters, one per corner.
- Rotating a footprint rotates its pieces: an L and its rotations produce
  the same pieces in rotated positions.

**`FrameTiling`**
- A rectangular board with no walls is framed on all four sides with outer
  corners at its four corners.
- A wall cell inside the board is drawn as frame, joined to the frame when
  it touches the edge.
- A notch in the board's outline produces a concave corner in the frame.

**`BoardLayout`**
- In a portrait aspect, the board plus frame fills the width minus the side
  margins, and the top and bottom bands stay free.
- In a landscape or very short aspect, the height decides and the whole
  board is visible.

**By hand (owner)**
- Every level looks right: blocks read as one piece, L shapes have a clean
  inside corner, walls read as frame, gates as coloured arrowed openings.
- Dragging, settling, clearing through a gate and peeling a layer all look
  right with the new pieces.
- Regenerating the art with an unchanged recipe changes no file.

## Resolved during implementation

- **HUD bands are screen fractions**, not cells. The HUD is a screen-space
  canvas, so a band in cells grew with the cell's size on screen: huge on a
  small board, too thin on a large one. `topBandScreenFraction` and
  `bottomBandScreenFraction` (defaults 0.14 and 0.10, summing below 1)
  replace them; the side margin stays in cells. The fit is
  `max(((W + 2t)/2 + side)·cs / aspect, (H + 2t)·cs/2 / (1 − top − bottom))`,
  and the camera, not the board, moves up by `(top − bottom)·size`, so the
  pointer's grid mapping is unchanged. The camera is fitted on every level
  load as well as on screen changes.
- **Pieces.** `QuarterTile` carries the pose: every sprite is drawn once for
  the top-right quarter, an edge along y is the edge sprite turned −90° with
  its flips swapped, since a sprite flips before it rotates.
  `QuarterTiling` holds the rule once for both tilings.
- **Frame.** `FrameTiling` frames the ring of cells just outside the grid,
  corners included, plus every wall cell, minus the ring cells under a
  gate's span; a gate's span is tiled as its own shape. The ring is squeezed
  to the frame thickness, and quarters that meet at a seam share their scale
  along it. A generator's bar is drawn over a continuous frame.
- **Art.** Eleven sprites: four quarter pieces, studs, stud gloss, floor,
  gate arrow, a 9-sliced axis arrow, background ramp and vignette. All are
  uncompressed RGBA32 without mipmaps, clamped and bilinear: block
  compression smears anti-aliased edges, and no block format suits both
  desktop and mobile WebGL. A corner's rounding stops half a pixel short of
  every seam, so neighbouring pieces line up; the recipe checks it.
- **Lip and peel.** The lip is a second, darkened set of quarters offset
  downward, sorted behind every face. A peel draws the exposed face beneath,
  recolours the lip at once, and lifts the old face above it with a
  `SortingGroup` while it shrinks away.
  *Later:* a block's lip offset downward hung below its footprint, over the
  frame and gates on the bottom row. A block's lip now fills its own
  quarters and the face is raised on its downward outline sides by the lip
  (`FaceShape`), so a resting block draws nothing outside its cells; studs,
  gloss and the axis arrow rise by half the lip. The frame, gate and machine
  lips still hang, never over a block. The gap between blocks was narrowed
  in `ArtRecipe`, closer to the reference. Module 20 has its own peel since.
- **Retired:** `BlockCellRects` and its tests, `cellGap`,
  `cameraMarginCells`, `edgeBarThickness`, `backgroundColor`,
  `emptyCellColor`, `wallColor` and `cellOrder`. `RuntimeConfig.asset` was
  reset to the new defaults.
- **Deferred to Phase 3's profile.** Separate textures batch poorly, and the
  board is rebuilt from scratch after every move with about ten renderers
  per block cell. Both are to be measured on WebGL before anything changes;
  a packed sheet with edge-extended padding and pooling or partial redraws
  are the candidate fixes.
