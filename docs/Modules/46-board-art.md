# Module 46 — Board art

**Assembly:** `GateRush.Editor` (the art generator), `GateRush.Runtime` (the board's views and `RuntimeConfig`), generated PNGs under `Assets/Art/Generated` (tests in `GateRush.Tests`)
**Depends on:** Modules 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 45
**Phase:** 7.5

---

## Responsibility

Bring the board up to the interface's new look, as D79 decides. Module 45
redrew everything around the board; the board itself still has the flat
look of Phase 2, and next to the glossy interface it now reads as
unfinished. The board's art stays generated by code from one recipe
(D46), so it stays tunable, deterministic and tinted per colour; what
changes is what the recipe draws, matched to a target image made first.

In scope:

- A target image of the board, and one of the mechanics, generated by
  the owner from the prompts below and approved before any code. They are
  references only and never ship.
- The blocks: outline, face, lip, gloss and studs.
- The block palette, retuned to the interface's palette.
- The floor, the frame and the gates.
- The level's background gradient, kept plain but in the interface's
  purple family.
- The state visuals of every mechanic (D48): ice, count badges, chains,
  padlock and keys, shutters, generators, elevators, layered blocks,
  one-way arrows and the time-bonus mark.
- The board's effects that use its sprites: exit cubes, ice shards and
  the gate glow.

Not in scope: any rule, layout, size or timing; the level editor's own
drawing (`EditorBlockLook`), which keeps its look; the joker bar and the
HUD (Module 45); new effects and animation (7.6, Module 47); sound.

## The look

The style guide of Module 45 applies, with these board rules:

- **Outline.** Every block, the frame and every gate has the same dark
  purple outline, #2A1650, whatever its colour. It is never tinted by the
  block's colour.
- **Face and lip.** The face is the block's colour with a soft vertical
  gradient, lighter at the top; the lip is a darker shade of the same
  colour along the bottom edge only, so a block reads as raised toward
  the viewer. Both come from the greyscale sprites and the block's tint,
  as today.
- **Gloss.** A white band across the top of each block, never tinted, as
  the buttons have; the studs keep a small white shine each.
- **Studs.** One large stud per cell, raised and glossy, in the block's
  colour, as in the target (Module 15 had two by two).
- **One piece.** A block still reads as one piece across its cells, with
  no seams inside it (the quarter-tile shapes of Module 15).
- **Palette.** The block palette moves to the interface's family. The
  faces the player sees aim at: Red #F04B5A, Blue #36A3F0, Green #3CCB4A,
  Yellow #FFC531, Purple #9B5CF0, Orange #FF8C1A, Pink #FF6FB5, Cyan
  #2FD3E6. Every pair stays easy to tell apart, including for the
  common forms of colour blindness as far as the eight colours allow.
- **Frame and floor.** The frame like the interface's dark pill (face
  #3A2370, lip #241446, outline); the floor a deeper purple with a faint
  grid, so blocks stand out on it.
- **Gates.** Coloured openings in the frame in the same glossy style as
  the blocks, with a white arrow outlined in dark purple.
- **Background.** A plain vertical gradient in the interface's purple
  family, about #4A2A86 at the top to #1E1036 at the bottom, with the
  vignette as today. Plain, so the board stays the brightest thing on
  the screen (Module 45 kept the level free of paintings for this).
- **State visuals.** Each keeps its meaning (D48) and takes the outline,
  gloss and palette: glassy ice with white frost, the count badge in the
  interface's red badge style with a white outlined digit, grey chains
  with a gold padlock, a gold key with its lock's coloured gem (D47),
  shutters and lift doors in dark purple with an orange border.

## The measured targets

Measured from the approved targets (`docs/media/board-target.png`,
`docs/media/board-target-mechanics.png`); a cell there is about 102
pixels. Shares are of a cell's side. Where the target is a generated
picture and not a real board (its blocks do not sit on one grid, and
its gaps vary), the value chosen is given instead.

**Blocks**
- One stud per cell, centred, about 0.47 of the cell across, raised,
  with a darker ring at its foot and a small white shine at its top
  left. This replaces the two by two studs of Module 15.
- Outline: dark purple, about 0.04 of the cell, the same on every
  colour.
- Corner radius: about 0.2 of the cell, as today.
- Gloss: a light band along the top edge, about 0.12 of the cell, then
  a face that darkens slightly toward the bottom (on red, #FC6167 at the
  top to #F93239 at the bottom).
- Lip: a darker band along the bottom edge, about 0.18 of the cell (on
  red, #C01E26 to #AD1423), with a thin light line just above it.
- Gap between blocks: about 0.05 of the cell (chosen; today 0.02, the
  target's 0.1 or more is too loose for a packed board).

**Palette (the faces' middle)**
- Red #FC464E, Blue #16A0FE, Green #27CC42, Yellow #FECA24, Purple
  #A348FD, Orange #FC7505. Pink and Cyan are not in the target and keep
  the spec's #FF6FB5 and #2FD3E6, at the same brightness as the others.

**Board**
- Frame: face #492184, outline #1C0B3A, about 0.35 of a cell wide with
  a lighter inner rim.
- Floor between blocks: #281350, with a faint grid a little lighter.
- Background: #602DA0 at the top to #200E3C at the bottom, a little
  brighter than the look above asked; the target's values are used.
- Gates: as wide as today, drawn as glossy openings in the gate's
  colour with the blocks' gloss and lip, and a white arrow with a dark
  purple outline pointing out of the board. The target's small bars that
  stick out of the frame are not used.

**Mechanics**
- Ice: glassy and see-through, light blue with white cracks and frost
  over the block, the block's own studs visible beneath.
- Count badge: a red badge with a white digit outlined in dark purple,
  from the interface's `badge` sprite (the target's round badge is
  close enough to keep one badge across the game).
- Chains: light grey (#C9C8E2) links with a dark purple outline, laid
  out as today; the target's crossed chains are used only if the plan
  finds a clean way to draw them on any shape.
- Padlock: gold (#FFE041) with a keyhole and the keys still needed on
  its body.
- Key: gold with a round bow and a faceted gem in its lock's colour.
- Shutter: dark purple slats (#422C75) in an orange frame (#FD6801)
  with rivets at the corners and a yellow and black hazard stripe along
  its foot.
- Generator: a light grey metal box with rivets and a dark blue screen
  (#081B55) showing the next block.
- Elevator: dark purple doors (#422C71) with a centre seam, in an
  orange frame (#FB5900) with hazard stripes up its sides.
- One-way: a white double-headed arrow with a dark purple outline along
  the block's axis.
- Time bonus: a gold clock coin (#FED42A) with "+5" in white outlined
  digits beside it.

## Design decisions (owner)

- The board's sprites are still generated by `ArtGenerator` from
  `ArtRecipe` and committed as PNGs (D46). The target images are only a
  reference; nothing from them is cut or shipped.
- What can be tinted stays greyscale and tinted at runtime; what must not
  change with the colour (the outline, the gloss, the frost) is drawn
  untinted, as the stud gloss already is.
- Where the interface already has the same piece, the board may use the
  interface's sprite instead of generating its own: the red badge behind
  a count, and the lock if it reads well at board size. The plan says
  which, and the sprite stays in its Module 45 import settings.
- Colours stay in `RuntimeConfig`, sizes in `ArtRecipe`, and both are
  checked in their `Problems()`.
- The board's sizes, layout and every timing stay as they are; the board
  fit of Modules 40 and 45 is not touched.
- Performance: the board is drawn with no more draw calls than today on
  a full board, or the plan says why and by how many; the WebGL build is
  the target.
- The introduction cards draw the mechanics with the board's own art, so
  they follow the new look without a change of their own.

The work is done in passes, each played and reviewed by the owner with

## Tests

**`ArtRecipe`, `ArtGenerator`**
- Every new or changed parameter is checked in `Problems()`: sizes
  positive, shares within range, the outline narrower than the gap
  allows.
- The generator writes every sprite the board uses, at the recipe's
  sizes, and the same recipe gives the same pixels.

**`RuntimeConfig`**
- The palette has one entry per colour; the outline, gloss, frame, floor
  and background colours are present and opaque where they must be.

**By hand (owner), per pass**
- Every level of the campaign looks right beside the target: blocks read
  as raised single pieces with one outline colour, every colour is easy
  to tell apart, gates read as openings.
- Every mechanic's state is as clear as before in play and in its
  introduction card.
- The board is not slower on a phone in the web build.

## Resolved during implementation

- **The method.** The first pass drew the target's look from hand-placed
  bands (an outline, a lighter top, a lip, a gloss band); in play it
  read as artificial. The board is now shaded from a height field
  instead (D80), ported from a small Python reference render the owner
  approved before any code, and every later look was first mocked the
  same way.
- **The lit model.** A block's edge rises as a quarter circle over 16
  pixels to a height of 11 (at 128 pixels a cell). It is lit by one
  light from the top left, (-0.45, -0.55, 0.70), with ambient 0.60,
  diffuse 0.55 and a Blinn-Phong specular (shininess 30, strength 0.30),
  in linear space, with an analytic ambient occlusion. The face sprites
  carry the grey shade at half value; the `Gate Rush/Board Sprite` and
  `Gate Rush/Board UI Sprite` shaders double it and add the specular, so
  the tint still sets the colour. A comparison with the reference
  matched within 4 of 255 on 98.7 percent of pixels.
- **What the measured targets lost.** No outline, no lip and no gloss
  band: the light draws the edge. The gap between blocks is 6 pixels and
  the corner radius 24. A soft floor shadow sits under every raised body
  (blocks, frame, gates) in the slot the outline had.
- **Studs.** Two by two per cell after all, not the target's one: 35
  pixels across, 59 apart, flat-topped cylinders with a contact shadow
  and a specular rim.
- **Layered blocks.** The outer block is drawn darker (0.85) with a
  recessed window, inset 0.31 of a cell, that shows the colour beneath
  as a smaller lit body with its own studs.
- **Palette.** Measured from the reference look and kept: Red #F02A1E,
  Orange #F6660C, Yellow #F6BA00, Green #07A515, Cyan #06A8F6, Blue
  #0054F6, Purple #A236F6, Pink #F63CA2.
- **Frame and background.** The frame is #483CA8 on a background of
  #2A2460 to #161236, so it stands out from the level behind it.
- **Mechanics (pass 2).** Mocked in the same lit model and approved
  first. Parts of a fixed colour are baked in colour by Generate Art
  from the colours in `RuntimeConfig`; parts whose colour varies stay
  grey, tinted at runtime under a highlight. A frozen block's body is
  drawn in the ice colour, so its colour stays hidden until it thaws
  (M3). The board paints its own count badge (gold rim, red face) rather
  than using the interface's sprite. The key's gem is a smooth dome, not
  faceted. One-way blocks lose their studs and carry a raised arrow. The
  old Frost, Ring and DoorPanel sprites are gone.
- **The time bonus.** Redrawn after play: a clock coin with a green rim
  (#2BC23A) and dark green hands, then a white +N at the count digits'
  size with a 3-pixel dark outline, a 4-pixel gap between them. Coin and
  label are one group centred on the mark's anchor, so the coin moves
  with the text, and on a narrow block (a vertical 1x2, an L) the group
  shrinks to fit within the key's margin. The label uses its own outline
  font asset (padding 17, digits and plus only) and `LabelOutline.mat`,
  so the shared label font and its shadow are untouched. The first
  version drew the outline twice as wide: TMP's distance field spans two
  gradient scales per unit, not one.
- **Keys.** Drawn at 1/1.2 of their painted size by
  `RuntimeConfig.keyScale`, gem included.
- **Marks on L and T blocks.** They sit on the cell where the arms meet
  (`MarkLayout.TryJunction`), not on an end cell. Other shapes keep the
  old rule, and the corner badges keep their own.
- **Comparison commands.** *Gate Rush → Render Lighting Comparison*,
  *Render Mechanics Comparison* and *Render Time Bonus Comparison*
  render the generated art beside the references, written to a
  git-ignored folder.
- **Pass 3 became performance.** A move used to destroy and redraw the
  whole board. The floor and the frame are now drawn once per level and
  kept; a move redraws only what it can change, about a quarter fewer
  renderers per move on level 29. A Unity Sprite Atlas is not used: its
  padding is transparent, which would bring back seams on the quarter
  tiles; a generated sheet with edge-extended padding is the seam-safe
  route, left for the optimisation in 7.8. The board's effects (exit
  cubes, shards, the gate glow), and a fade for an elevator that has
  finished its waves, move to 7.6 (Module 47).
- **The joker bar.** Reworked here: a tight group in the centre with a
  quarter button between buttons, a dark pill under each button for its
  count, price or level, and the icon at 185 units.
- **Tests.** 2,882 after pass 1, 3,012 at the end.
